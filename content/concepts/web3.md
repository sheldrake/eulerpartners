# [Concepts](/concepts/) / Web3

The web originally required technical skills to publish anything to it. Retrospectively, this stage has been called Web 1. Then came Web 2 (aka the [social](/concepts/social/) web, the _read + write_ web), enabling anyone anywhere to publish anything.

A vision for the web known as the semantic web (see [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Web)) was and is still referred to as Web3. Confusingly then, all things relating to what many refer to simply as "crypto" has also attracted the moniker.

Ironically for a field obsessed with [distributed](/concepts/distributed/) consensus, there is no consensus on defining this more recent Web3. Here's one way ...

Web3 is a subset of the decentralized web ([dweb](/concepts/dweb/)) describing the relatively recent application of novel cryptographic techniques to the challenges of [distributed computing](/concepts/distributed-computing/). [Distributed ledger](/concepts/distributed-ledger/) technology is a subset of Web3, of which [blockchain](/concepts/blockchain/) is one form, and so [cryptocurrency](/concepts/cryptocurrency/) and [tokens](/concepts/token/) are very much in the mix. [Information-centric networking](/concepts/info-centric-networking/) (e.g. IPFS, Hypercore, Maidsafe, and Swarm) and so-called self-sovereign [identity](/concepts/identity/) (SSI) also come under the Web3 banner.

It's become popular to think of the newer Web3 in terms of _read + write + own._ Nevertheless, putting property on such a pedestal is not entirely appropriate, or indeed imaginative. Rather, it may be thought of in terms of commoning, of cooperating, of helping us work better together at scale.
