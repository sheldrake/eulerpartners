# [Concepts](/concepts/) / Public goods

Public goods are [non-rivalrous](/concepts/rivalry/) resources that no-one can be excluded from using. They are also known as [social](/concepts/social/) goods and collective goods. Oft-cited examples include street lighting, broadcast TV, knowledge, and the World Wide Web.

Non-proprietary [social networking](/concepts/social-networking/) that is open to anyone is a public good. The resources needed to establish [decentralized](/concepts/decentralized/) social (dsocial) networking may be a public good when the corresponding resources have [open source](/concepts/open-source-software/) / [open design](/concepts/open-design/) licenses.

Hart et al (2021) are optimistic for [Web3](/concepts/web3/) public goods:

> Greater scale should mean greater good as valued by an increasingly wider set of people: the creation of positive externalities.
