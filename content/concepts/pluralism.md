# [Concepts](/concepts/) / Pluralism

Glen Weyl (2022) describes pluralism as a [social](/concepts/social/) philosophy that recognizes and fosters the flourishing of and cooperation between a diversity of social groups / systems.

Classical pluralism studies the distribution of power and influence in a political process. Its objects of study include interest groups, conflict, and bargaining processes, with an emphasis on diversity, slow change to maintain a democratic equilibrium, and individual goal attainment.

Value pluralism (aka [ethical](/concepts/ethics-of-technology/) pluralism, moral pluralism) recognises the co-existence of irreconcilable values.

Pluralism’s heterogeneity is related to [variety](/concepts/variety/) and so to [viability](/concepts/viability/).
